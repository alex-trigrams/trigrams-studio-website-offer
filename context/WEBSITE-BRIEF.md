# Website brief: TRIGRAMS 1.0 to 2.0

Handoff doc for Claude Code. August 2026. All decisions in this doc are settled unless marked otherwise.

**Scope:** messaging and page structure. Keep the existing visual language, components and Tailwind tokens. Change what pages exist, what order things appear in, and what the words say. This is not a redesign.

---

## 1. What changed in the business

The site currently sells a solo operator with a menu of services. The business is now a growth infrastructure company that assembles capability. Same person, different machine.

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| What's sold | Websites, ads, content, video, as a menu | Growth Systems, one outcome |
| Who delivers | Alex, personally | A network of specialists. Alex is accountable. |
| Trust signal | "One person, no handoffs" | One person accountable, a bench behind them |
| Pricing shown | From $500 + GST | Projects from $5,000 |
| Revenue model | Per service | Per system, priced to carry partner margin |

The promise: **we build predictable systems that generate attention, leads and conversions.**

Clients buy leads, growth, revenue and time back. The services are the tooling that gets them there.

---

## 2. The positioning decision

This is the single most important change on the site, so it gets its own section.

**The site now says delivery is partnered.** Alex is the strategist and the single point of contact. Specialists build. This is true, it's a stronger story than one person doing everything, and it's what makes a $5,000 project make sense.

The line to replace "one person, no handoffs" needs to carry three things:

1. You deal with one person, always
2. That person is not personally doing every task
3. That's the upside, not the compromise

Direction for the copy: name the accountability and the network in the same breath. Something in the shape of *"One person accountable. A network of specialists behind them."* Claude Code should write three or four options in this territory, not copy that line verbatim.

**What this changes elsewhere:**

- **About page.** The story becomes: started in video, learned what actually moves the needle, now assembles the people who build it. The 5 years at a managed service provider is real credibility for running systems, use it.
- **FAQ.** The "is this a one person operation" answer flips. New answer: one person you deal with, a network doing the work, and that's why the output isn't capped by one person's calendar.
- **Capacity language.** Remove all of it. No waitlists, no "at capacity", no "limited spots". The whole point of the new model is that capacity is bought, not rationed.

## 4. Proposed structure

Nav drops to five: The System, Work, About, How It Works, Book a call.

### Homepage

1. **Hero.** Keep the headline. Rewrite the subhead around the system.
2. **Proof, promoted.** The $6.28 gym number, one stat, given real room. Above the fold or immediately under it.
3. **Which one sounds like you?** Four problem cards, each mapping to a layer. No attention, no traffic, no conversion, no follow-up.
4. **The growth system.** New section, and the most important block on the site. The four layers shown as one connected thing, not four separate cards. The connection is the product.
5. **How it works.** Existing content pulled up from its own page. Three or four steps.
6. **How the work gets done.** New short block. This is where the network story lives on the homepage. Two or three sentences.
7. **Who it's for.** Real estate, allied health, fitness, service businesses. Specific beats broad.
8. **About, condensed.** Short block, full story on its own page.
9. **CTA.** Book a discovery call.

### The System (replaces Services)

One page, four layers, in order.

- **Layer 1, Attention.** Content, photography, video, social proof.
- **Layer 2, Traffic.** Meta ads, creative testing, campaign strategy.
- **Layer 3, Conversion.** Landing pages, websites, conversion tracking.
- **Layer 4, Follow up.** CRM, automation, lead nurture.

Each layer gets three things: what it does, what breaks without it, what you actually receive.

No per-layer prices. Layers are not individually purchasable. Do not label which layers are partner-delivered, that level of detail is internal.

### Work

**Read this section carefully. There is very little real proof and none of it can be invented.**

**Case study 1: the Perth gym.** The only engagement with recorded performance data. Build it out properly: the problem, the system built, the number. Structure: 54 enquiries, 30 days, $6.28 each.

**Pulse Property Group and Waterford Wellness Centre appear as journey stories only.** These describe the work and what it was for. They carry **zero** performance claims, and no numbers are being chased for them. Write them as "here's what we built and why", not as a results case study with the results missing.

Verified facts that may be used:

*Pulse Property Group, boutique real estate, Shelley and Rossmoyne WA.* 20 reels per month. Two full-day shoots a month. Instagram feed 3 to 4 posts weekly, stories 2 to 3 weekly. Meta ads campaign build and management. Social media management. Content reporting. Ran May to June 2026.

*Waterford Wellness Centre, allied health clinic, Manning.* Audit, content pillars, 2-week content calendar, 4 organic posts, email capture live on the site. May 2026. No paid ads.

**Hard constraints on this page:**

- Do not use lead counts, cost per lead, reach, follower growth, appraisals booked, conversion rates or engagement lifts for Pulse or Waterford. None exist. The trackers are empty.
- Do not use the 246 RateMyAgent reviews. They're Zac's, they pre-date the engagement, and using them implies attribution that isn't there.
- Do not use RateMyAgent review quotes as testimonials. They're reviews of the agent's property work, not of the marketing.
- Do not use Waterford's fee ($400) anywhere.
- No AHPRA-regulated health claims or patient testimonials for Waterford. Compliance risk.
- If a number isn't in this brief, it doesn't exist. Ask, don't estimate.

### About

Rewrite around the shift: started in video, learned what makes marketing work, now builds the systems and assembles the people who deliver them. 5 years at a managed service provider before that. This is where the network story gets its fullest telling.

### Retire

**Newsletter as a nav item.** Signup stays in the footer. It hasn't earned a top-level slot.

## 7. Copy direction

Follow `docs/anti-ai-writing-style.md`. The rules that get broken most:

- No em dashes.
- No negative parallelism. Never "it's not about X, it's about Y." Just say what it is.
- Sentence case headers.
- Contractions. Digits for numbers. Short paragraphs.

Brand voice: a partner sitting next to you, not an agency pitching at you. Plain English, specific numbers, direct.

**Use:** system, leads, revenue, ads, ship, deliver, repeatable, simple.
**Never use:** unlock, leverage, synergy, transform, elevate, game-changer, bespoke, world-class.

Test for every block: could a small business owner read this on their phone in 30 seconds and know what to do next.
